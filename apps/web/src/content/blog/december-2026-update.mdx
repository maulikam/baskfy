Three things change in December 2026: what the product covers, what it costs, and what it admits
it cannot do yet. This page is the whole of it, in that order.

## What is being added

**A longer price history.** Published factor history currently starts on 1 November 2024, which
bounds every historical screen and every backtest. The backfill to 2011 is the largest outstanding
piece of work in the build and is what the December release is organised around. Until it lands, a
backtest requesting an earlier start date fails with a clear message rather than silently
truncating to whatever exists.

**Backtests over real history.** The engine, the metrics, the fragility runs and the artefact
export are all built and tested. They currently run against a synthetic market because there is no
real history to run them against. The backfill is what turns them on.

**Dividend-adjusted totals throughout.** Cash dividends are already folded into the adjustment
factor, which makes every factor a total-return figure. What is not built is a dividend-stripped
series, which is why the backtest engine refuses `dividends: "cash"` and `dividends: "ignore"`
rather than quietly serving the reinvested variant under a different name.

## What the prices become

{props.priceTable}

Every price is inclusive of GST. Existing subscriptions are not repriced mid-term: a yearly plan
bought before the change renews at the new price at its next renewal, and a Forever plan bought
before the change is not affected by it at all — that is what buying it means.

The pricing page reads these numbers from the billing service, not from this page, so what it
shows is what will actually be charged.

## What "Forever" means

The lifetime of the service, not the lifetime of the buyer. It is stated at the point of sale, in
the terms, and here, because a one-time price on a subscription product has exactly one honest
reading and three dishonest ones.

## What is still not solved

- **Fundamentals.** Nothing ingests them, so the P/E column and the P/E filter are empty for every
  instrument. This is not scheduled for December.
- **The risk-free rate.** Backtest Sharpe and Sortino figures are excess over zero until a
  Treasury-bill series exists. The number on the page is correct for what it measures and is not
  the number a performance report would quote.
- **The public API.** Built, tested, and deliberately switched off pending a written
  data-redistribution opinion. It is not a launch date question.
- **Refunds.** `refund` events from the payment gateway are acknowledged and otherwise ignored;
  there is no refund endpoint. The refund policy sets out how a refund is actually handled today,
  which is by hand.

None of this is a roadmap commitment. It is a list of things that are wrong, published because a
tool that hides its limits is asking to be trusted further than it has earned.
