Momentum screening has one honest claim and a great many dishonest ones. The honest claim is
arithmetic: over a stated window, on a stated price series, these names moved further than those
names. Everything past that is inference, and this post is about the arithmetic.

## The window is a calendar window, not a bar count

A one-year return in Baskfy is the change from the adjusted close on the last trading day on or
before the same date one calendar year earlier. Not 252 bars back. Not 250. The distinction
matters more than it sounds: an instrument that was suspended for three weeks has fewer bars in
its year than one that traded every session, and counting bars would quietly compare a 12-month
move against an 11-month one.

The consequence is that the number of trading days inside each window varies by instrument and by
date. On the reference date this build was verified against, the five windows resolve to 22, 64,
121, 185 and 247 trading days.

## The price series is adjusted, and the price column is not

Every factor reads an adjusted close: splits and bonuses folded into a single multiplicative
factor, so a 1:10 split is not a 90% drawdown. Cash dividends are deliberately *not* folded in, so
every number here is a price return — about 1.2% a year below a total return on NSE, compounding,
and a dividend does show as a small gap down on its ex-date. The
price you see in the price column, and the price the CMP filter compares against, is the exchange
print — because that is the number on your broker's screen and a filter that disagreed with it
would be useless.

Both are stored. Neither is derived on the fly.

## Sharpe here is not the Sharpe in a textbook

The Sharpe return factors divide the window's return by the realised volatility of daily log
returns over that same window, annualised. There is no risk-free rate in it. That is a deliberate
simplification of a ranking key, not an oversight, and it is worth being explicit about because
the same word means something stricter in a performance-attribution context.

The practical effect is that these are *ratios for sorting*, and comparing one across two
different windows is comparing two different quantities.

## A null is not a zero

An instrument that lacks a full window has no value for that factor. It is null, and a null
satisfies no predicate — so a stock listed four months ago is excluded by any one-year filter
rather than ranked last with a zero. It is absent from the ranking, not at the bottom of it.

This is the single most common source of "why is this stock missing", and it is almost always the
right answer.

## What none of this tells you

Nothing here is a view. A ranking says where a name sits on one measurement on one date. It does
not say the measurement will persist, that the ranking is a portfolio, or that the top of it is a
good place to be. Baskfy is not a SEBI-registered investment adviser and publishes no advice; what
it publishes is the arithmetic, in enough detail that you can disagree with it precisely.
